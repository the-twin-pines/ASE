# Identify the assist architecture first

Scope: architecture only. Sources: bosch-eps-architecture; zf-ehps-noise.
Bosch's operating principle separates torque input, controller and assist motor,
with column and rack variants. ZF's EPHS article describes an electric motor-pump
feeding a hydraulic gear. Engine-driven hydraulic steering uses a mechanical pump
drive. These distinctions determine which tests have meaning.

An EPS-only car has no hydraulic steering reservoir or pump-pressure test.
An EHPS pump can run independently of engine speed while still using fluid.
An internal EPS belt does not imply a hydraulic pump. Confirm the installed
system through exact service information before selecting a test. Preserve
mechanical binding and tire/load causes in every architecture. No part replacement
or numerical service limit follows from this architecture page.

# Heavy parking steering that improves with engine speed

Synthetic trace, engine-driven hydraulic system only; source trw-1250-2002,
test 5. Sequence: confirm architecture; record cold/hot, idle/moving and engine-
speed behavior; inspect fluid/drive and tire/load conditions. Competing hypotheses:
insufficient pump delivery versus excessive load/gear leakage. Next discriminator:
technician comparison of pump flow and relief performance under the applicable
service conditions. Deficient supply raises pump/drive/feed suspicion; adequate
supply moves attention downstream. RPM improvement alone does not prove a worn
pump. Do not transfer the commercial manual's numbers to a car.

# EPS warning and intermittent assist with other electrical symptoms

Synthetic trace; source gm-18-na-161-v4, pages 1–4. Match the model/year table
and LD EPS qualification before applying the bulletin. Sequence: preserve codes,
event conditions and other blanking/stall symptoms; measure loaded supply/ground

ground resistance versus a local torque-input/control/motor fault. Discriminator:
loaded voltage-drop observation with the bulletin's applicable SI method.
An excessive localized drop ranks that connection above a speculative rack
replacement; satisfactory power leaves input, communication, control and motor
tests pending. Resting battery voltage or a restart does not prove the supply good.
