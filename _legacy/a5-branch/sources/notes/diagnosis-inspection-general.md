## Study spine

- Describe the symptom first: pedal travel, pedal effort, brake force, pull, noise, vibration, heat, warning lamp, or failure to release.
- Then record conditions: hot/cold, first stop/repeated stops, engine on/off, after pumping, straight-line/turning, one wheel/all wheels.
- Use discriminating tests rather than parts guesses: pumping the pedal, holding steady pressure, comparing wheel temperatures, checking free rotation, opening a bleeder on a dragging corner, measuring runout, and comparing live wheel-speed data.
- A vehicle pulling under braking usually means unequal brake force; the fault may be on either side.
- Contamination from axle seals, grease or brake fluid changes friction and can mimic a purely mechanical brake fault.
- Some playlist entries are useful adjacent material rather than core light-vehicle brake content; keep that distinction visible.
