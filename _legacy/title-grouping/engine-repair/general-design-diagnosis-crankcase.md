# General engine design, diagnosis and crankcase ventilation

Source grouping is based on titles/metadata only.

## General study notes — not extracted claims from the videos

- Engine families can have recurring mechanical failure patterns, but a known pattern is a hypothesis generator, not a substitute for testing the individual engine.
- A misfire can arise from compression/mechanical condition as well as ignition or fuel. A1-relevant work is mainly the mechanical side: compression, leakage, valve train, sealing and cylinder condition.
- Positive crankcase ventilation controls blow-by flow and crankcase pressure. Restrictions or uncontrolled air paths can contribute to leaks, contamination and drivability symptoms.
- “Small block” and “big block” are historical engine-family labels, not universal dimensional categories.
- General mechanism explanations are useful for connecting piston motion, crankshaft motion, valve timing, combustion and lubrication before learning specific repair procedures.
