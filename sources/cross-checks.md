# A4 cross-check sources

These are not substitutes for the reviewed videos. They exist to stop a useful demonstration from becoming an overgeneralized rule.

## Ball-joint inspection loading

MOOG — Signs of Loose Ball Joints  
https://www.moogparts.com/technical/bulletins/tech-tips/how-to-inspect-ball-joints-for-looseness.html

Useful correction: inspection depends on whether the joint is load-carrying, follower/non-load-carrying, or part of a MacPherson-strut arrangement. The page gives different unloading and pry/dial-indicator procedures rather than one universal wheel-shake rule.

Keep from this: when answering a real vehicle question, identify the suspension design or consult its service procedure before saying exactly where to jack or how much play is allowed.

## Shock/strut weepage versus leakage

Monroe — Shock Weepage vs Leakage  
https://www.monroe.com/technical-resources/servicegrams/weepage-vs-leakage-shocks-struts.html

Useful correction: a light film/weepage can be acceptable. Broad wetness or fluid dripping from the unit is treated as leakage and a replacement condition.

Keep from this: do not diagnose a failed strut solely from “there is some oil on it.” Describe the extent of fluid and combine it with ride-control symptoms and physical inspection.

## Shock/strut inspection as a whole-system check

Monroe — How to Conduct a Shock or Strut Inspection  
https://www.monroe.com/technical-resources/tech-tips/how-to-conduct-a-shock-strut-inspection.html

The useful order is customer/repair history → road evaluation → physical inspection. Look for ride/control change, bounce/float, dive/squat/roll, rough-road stability, noises, physical damage, leakage, bushing/mount problems, tire wear, and relevant electronic faults.

Keep from this: the bounce test is one clue inside a larger inspection, not a definitive oracle.

## Hunter vehicle-alignment training supplement

Hunter Engineering — Vehicle Alignment Seminar, Technician Reference Guide, 2008–2011  
https://bccskillsusa.weebly.com/uploads/4/3/3/2/43327659/hec_alignment_manual_edu_2.pdf

This is older training material, not current vehicle service information. It is useful because it spells out geometry and diagnostic relationships that are only named in several queued Hunter videos.

Keep from this:

- alignment changes can come from spring sag, load change, or worn/damaged steering and suspension parts;
- the road test should separate steady pull from braking pull, acceleration/deceleration pull, memory steer, and bump-related direction change;
- tire conicity/ply-steer and brake/steering faults remain competing causes of pull;
- frame angle is a front-to-rear frame-slope measurement used for frame-corrected caster on vehicles whose procedure requires it;
- caster is obtained by steering through a measurement sweep, not by treating a straight-ahead inclinometer value as caster;
- for the supplement's total four-wheel procedure the sequence is rear camber, rear toe/thrust, front caster, front camber, then front toe;
- SAI and included angle are diagnostic measurements that can help direct inspection toward bent/displaced suspension or structural parts;
- rear thrust angle remains diagnostically meaningful even if rear toe is not adjustable.

Do **not** turn its example numerical thresholds, model examples, or 2011-era workflow into universal specifications. Current vehicle service information controls.

## Hunter current alignment and collision material

Hunter Engineering — HawkEye Elite / collision alignment / alignment benefits  
https://www.hunter.com/alignment-machines/hawkeye-elite/  
https://www.hunter.com/collision  
https://www.hunter.com/cs/media-center/industry-insight/benefits-of-wheel-alignment/

Useful cross-checks:

- current four-wheel systems report camber, caster, and toe for all four wheels where applicable;
- current collision-alignment material explicitly uses live ride height, toe-out-on-turns, maximum steering angle, symmetry/setback, and suspension-body-dimension information to uncover geometry problems;
- steering-system resets and ADAS calibration may be required after alignment according to vehicle/OEM information;
- Hunter continues to describe caster chiefly in terms of directional stability/returnability and total toe as a critical tire-wear/alignment measurement.

Keep from this: an alignment rack can be a diagnostic geometry tool, not merely an adjustment machine.

## QuickTrick written home-alignment procedure

QuickTrick Alignment — Alignment 101  
https://quicktrickalignment.com/alignment-101/

This is useful as a cross-check for the queued Fast Monty's Garage/QuickTrick video because the written procedure is directly inspectable.

Keep from this:

- check tire pressure and settle the suspension before measuring;
- establish the gauge's level reference;
- measure caster by steering through equal angles on both sides of center;
- use the order caster → camber → toe;
- when toe needs adjustment, establish an external/string reference so total toe alone does not hide which side is wrong.

Limitation: a portable home setup does not automatically supply rear thrust, SAI/included angle, setback/symmetry, runout compensation, or electronic reset/calibration information. State what the setup actually measures.
