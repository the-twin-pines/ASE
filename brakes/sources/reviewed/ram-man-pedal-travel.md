## Source-supported mechanisms

The Ram Man disc-brake service material describes **pad knock-back**: disc movement can push pads and pistons farther back, so the next application needs more hydraulic displacement before pad contact. The document connects this to hard cornering, loose or worn suspension/wheel-bearing parts, and excessive disc lateral runout. It separately notes that disc thickness variation can be associated with a pumping or pulsating pedal.

The Ram Man power-booster guide treats assist as a different diagnostic family. Its booster test is about whether power assist arrives; it does not turn a travel complaint into an assist complaint.

The transferable distinctions are:

- **assist faults** primarily change pedal effort;
- **clearance or knock-back** can increase pedal travel;
- **air/vapor or leakage** can also increase travel through different mechanisms;
- multiple faults can coexist.

The booster guide also points to rear brake-shoe adjustment when pedal travel is very low on the older systems it discusses. Keep that as application-specific service guidance rather than a universal measurement. The general lesson is that excessive shoe-to-drum clearance consumes hydraulic displacement.

## ASE study frame

For a low/long pedal, keep these observations separate: first-application height, change after pumping, firmness versus sponginess, whether the pedal holds position under steady force, and whether the complaint changes with assist. Then map those observations to clearance, compressibility, pressure-holding, or assist mechanisms instead of naming a part from the symptom alone.
